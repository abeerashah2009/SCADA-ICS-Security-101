# Task 3.1 — ICS/SCADA Physical Security Policy

## 1. Purpose

This policy establishes minimum physical security requirements for protecting ICS/SCADA equipment from unauthorized access, tampering, environmental hazards, and physical disruption.

The objective is to protect the confidentiality, integrity, availability, and safety of industrial control systems.

---

## 2. Scope

This policy applies to physical infrastructure supporting ICS/SCADA operations, including:

- PLCs
- RTUs
- HMIs
- Engineering workstations
- Industrial network equipment
- Control cabinets
- Communication equipment
- Remote control equipment
- Environmental monitoring systems
- Supporting power and cooling infrastructure

---

## 3. Physical Access Control

Access to critical ICS/SCADA equipment areas should be restricted to authorized personnel.

Controls may include:

- Mechanical locks
- Access cards
- Badge readers
- PIN authentication
- Biometric controls where appropriate
- Security personnel
- CCTV monitoring
- Visitor logging

Access permissions should be reviewed periodically.

---

## 4. Asset Protection

Critical equipment should be installed in appropriately secured locations.

Physical protection should include:

- Secure cabinets
- Tamper-evident seals where appropriate
- Protected cable entries
- Secure mounting
- Asset identification
- Appropriate enclosure protection
- Protection from unauthorized modification

Equipment should not be left exposed in publicly accessible areas.

---

## 5. Environmental Protection

Critical ICS/SCADA equipment should be protected against environmental conditions that could affect operational reliability.

Relevant conditions include:

- Dust
- Heat
- Humidity
- Moisture
- Vibration
- Water leakage
- Poor ventilation

Environmental monitoring should be implemented where operational risk requires it.

---

## 6. Inspection and Maintenance

Physical security inspections should be performed according to organizational requirements.

Inspections should verify:

- Enclosure condition
- Lock condition
- Tamper seals
- Cable entries
- Signs of unauthorized access
- Environmental conditions
- Equipment mounting
- Cooling systems
- Physical asset identification

Inspection results should be documented.

---

## 7. Tamper Detection

Evidence of unauthorized access should be treated as a security event.

Examples include:

- Broken seals
- Damaged locks
- Open cabinets
- Unknown cables
- Unauthorized devices
- Missing equipment
- Unexpected hardware modifications

Security personnel and operations personnel should be notified according to the incident-response procedure.

---

## 8. Environmental Monitoring

Where required, facilities should monitor:

- Temperature
- Humidity
- Vibration
- Water leakage
- Dust or contamination
- Power quality

Alerts should be configured according to equipment and process requirements.

---

## 9. Change Management

Physical modifications affecting ICS/SCADA equipment should follow approved change-management procedures.

Examples include:

- Installing new environmental sensors
- Modifying cabinets
- Changing cooling systems
- Installing access-control systems
- Changing cable routing
- Moving critical equipment

Changes should be documented, reviewed, approved, and verified.

---

## 10. Personnel Responsibilities

### Operations

Operations personnel should:

- Maintain awareness of physical security conditions.
- Report suspected tampering.
- Follow approved access procedures.
- Protect operational availability and safety.

### Security Personnel

Security personnel should:

- Manage physical access controls.
- Investigate physical security events.
- Maintain access records.
- Coordinate incident response.

### Maintenance Personnel

Maintenance personnel should:

- Perform approved maintenance.
- Document physical changes.
- Report damaged equipment.
- Follow change-management procedures.

---

## 11. Audit Requirements

Physical security controls should be periodically reviewed.

Audit evidence may include:

- Physical inspection records
- Access logs
- Maintenance records
- Tamper-seal records
- Environmental monitoring records
- Incident reports
- Change-management records

---

## 12. Policy Compliance

Failure to follow physical security requirements may increase the risk of:

- Unauthorized access
- Equipment tampering
- Operational disruption
- Equipment damage
- Safety incidents
- Loss of system availability

Exceptions should be documented and approved according to organizational procedures.

---

## AWS Lab Limitation

This policy was developed in an AWS EC2 environment.

The lab environment does not contain physical PLC cabinets, industrial control rooms, physical access-control systems, or industrial environmental monitoring equipment.

Therefore, this document represents a policy-development exercise rather than a claim of physical implementation.
